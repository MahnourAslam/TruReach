Here's the actual landscape of how creators get paid, broken into the models brands use:

1. Flat/fixed fee (most common for brand deals)

A set price per deliverable, agreed before anything is posted. Usually based on:

Follower count / average views — often via a rough CPM: (expected views ÷ 1,000) × agreed CPM rate
Engagement rate — a smaller but highly engaged audience can command more than a bigger passive one
Niche/vertical — beauty, finance, tech creators often price differently than lifestyle
Platform — TikTok vs Instagram vs YouTube have different going rates
Content type — a single Story is cheaper than a dedicated video; a 60-sec integration costs more than a group/brand-tag post
Usage rights & exclusivity — can the brand reuse the content in ads? Is the creator barred from competitor deals for a period? Both push price up
2. Performance-based / affiliate

Creator earns per outcome, not per post:

Commission per sale (affiliate link or unique discount code)
Cost-per-click on a tracked link
Bonuses for hitting view or sales thresholds
This is how brands measure ROI after the post — promo codes are one of the most common attribution tools
3. Hybrid (increasingly the norm)

Flat fee + performance kicker — creator gets guaranteed pay regardless, plus upside if the content actually converts. This is brands hedging: they pay for reach up front, but reward real results on top.

4. Platform-native monetization (separate from brand deals)
Creator funds / bonus programs (TikTok Creativity Program, YouTube ad revenue, Instagram bonuses) — paid directly by the platform based on views/watch time, not brand-negotiated
Subscriptions/fan support (Patreon, channel memberships, TikTok gifts) — fans pay directly
The gap your idea sits in

Notice: none of these payment models actually verify what happened inside the video. Flat fees are priced on expected reach before the post exists. Performance-based pay tracks sales, not integration quality. Nobody's pricing model currently asks "did the creator actually say the brand name, show the product clearly, and write it in the caption — or just flash it once?" That's the exact gap TrueReach fills: not replacing these payment models, but adding a missing verification layer that sits between the flat fee and the performance metrics — proving whether the flat fee was justified by what was actually delivered.
