Yeah — referral codes are one part of it, but they’re mostly for measuring sales/conversions after the campaign, not usually the only thing determining the creator’s fee.

Right now brands usually think about influencer payment in a few buckets:

Upfront fixed fee — based on follower size, average views, engagement rate, niche, platform, and the type of content they’re making.
Performance-based — commission per sale, unique discount-code usage, affiliate-link clicks/conversions, sometimes bonuses for hitting view/sales targets.
Hybrid — fixed fee + commission/bonus. This is increasingly common. 
Then there are extras like usage rights, exclusivity, how long the brand can reuse the content, number of posts, etc. 
And after the post is live, they typically judge it using things like views/reach, engagement, clicks, conversions, promo-code sales and ROI. Promo/discount codes are actually one of the most widely used attribution methods.

How pricing usually happens now
There isn’t one clean formula. Brands usually agree a base fee before the video exists, based on things like:

Expected views/reach + engagement + creator niche/audience + deliverables + usage rights + exclusivity.

Some brands/agencies use a rough CPM model:

expected views ÷ 1,000 × agreed CPM

Others negotiate from the creator’s rate card. Then you can add performance commission through affiliate links/codes or bonuses. 

So importantly:

They are mainly pricing the creator and expected distribution — not precisely pricing what happens inside the video.

And that is where your idea could slot in.
